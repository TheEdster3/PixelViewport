# Design-partner playbook

## Who to approach

Look for teams already paying the cost: .NET machine builders, vision system integrators, instrument vendors, and industrial software OEMs with WPF/WinUI/Avalonia interfaces and live or high-bit-depth images. The best first contact is a technical lead who owns delivery pain and can introduce the budget owner.

## Twenty-minute discovery outline

1. “Walk me through the last image-viewer problem your team had to solve.”
2. “What acquisition SDK, formats, resolutions, FPS, and target hardware are involved?”
3. “Where do latency, dropped frames, overlays, coordinates, or buffer ownership fail today?”
4. “What did the workaround cost in engineering time or deployment risk?”
5. “What would a credible evaluation have to prove?”
6. “If it met those criteria, who would approve a $2,500 pilot and an annual SDK license?”

Do not lead with a feature tour. Ask about specific past behavior, existing spend, urgency, and the buying process.

## Pilot invitation

> We are validating PixelViewport Vision, a vendor-neutral .NET display and interaction SDK for live/high-bit-depth imaging. We can run a four-week paid integration around one of your real workloads, with success criteria agreed in advance. The $2,500 pilot fee is credited toward the first annual license. If the target is not technically plausible after an initial review, we will say so before accepting the pilot.

## Tonight versus later

Tonight: establish the product contract, demo, tests, benchmark, docs, CI, target-account rubric, interview script, and evidence ledger.

Later, with humans available: source warm introductions, conduct discovery, qualify technical fit, obtain legal/payment details, and sign the first pilot. A design partnership cannot be honestly manufactured from more code alone.
