# Commercial operating model

PixelViewport should earn adoption through public MIT contracts and evaluation adapters, then charge for production performance, ROI workflow depth, source access, integration confidence, and support.

## Offer ladder

| Offer | Working price | Buyer outcome |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Community | Free | Validate API fit and prototype a viewport |
| Design Partner | $2,500, credited toward first license | Integrate one real workload with direct engineering access |
| Professional | $999/developer/year | Ship the production renderer and ROI toolkit with updates/support |
| Team | $3,999/year, five developers | Team entitlement plus onboarding |
| Enterprise | From $10,000/year | Redistribution/offline terms and prioritized support |

These are test prices, not published promises. The first three design-partner agreements should teach us whether the problem, scope, and price are real.

## What a paid design partner is

A design partner is a company with an active imaging problem that pays a modest, time-boxed fee to integrate an early build. In return it gets early access, an agreed integration outcome, direct support, and influence over the API. The fee filters casual interest and can be credited toward its first annual license.

It is not an investor, a testimonial swap, open-ended custom consulting, or a prerequisite to making progress tonight. We can prepare the demo, technical proof, target list, discovery script, and agreement outline first; outreach and calls happen when a human buyer is available.

## Proposed pilot shape

- Four weeks, one application, one image source, one named technical owner per side.
- Success criteria written before work begins (for example: `2048×2048 Gray16 at 30 FPS with p95 presentation age below an agreed threshold on named hardware`).
- $2,500 fee paid up front and credited toward the first Team or Enterprise license.
- General-purpose improvements may enter the product; customer code, images, and confidential details stay private.
- Anything outside the agreed workload requires a new scope.

Use counsel-approved commercial, confidentiality, privacy, warranty, export, and medical/safety terms before accepting money. The repository contains product guidance, not legal advice.

## Checkout rule

Do not enable self-serve checkout yet. The alpha does not have a proven production renderer or finalized commercial terms. The website should route qualified prospects to an evaluation/design-partner conversation and give developers a frictionless Community proof.

## Evidence ledger

For every conversation, record:

- customer segment and role;
- current camera/vision stack and UI framework;
- resolution, pixel format, frame rate, overlay load, and target hardware;
- the cost of the current workaround;
- urgency, procurement path, and budget owner;
- exact success criteria;
- commitment level: introduction, evaluation, pilot fee, license, renewal.

Product priorities come from repeated paid constraints, not feature-vote totals.
